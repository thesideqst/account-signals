Revenue for the quarter came in at $96.221 billion. That is up 17.9 percent quarter over quarter and up 105.9 percent year over year. Cost of revenue was $24.079 billion, rising 17.7 percent quarter over quarter and 86.8 percent year over year. Gross profit therefore stood at $72.142 billion, a rise of 18.0 percent quarter over quarter and 113.1 percent year over year. Operating income was $63.734 billion, up 19.0 percent quarter over quarter and 124.1 percent year over year. Net income reached $59.688 billion, up 2.3 percent quarter over quarter and 125.9 percent year over year. GAAP and non‑GAAP gross margins were both 75 percent, essentially flat from the prior quarter.

Management opened the call by calling the quarter “another outstanding quarter with record revenue, operating income and EPS.” The numbers back that claim. Revenue more than doubled versus the same quarter a year ago, and operating income grew by more than 124 percent year over year. Colette Kress said the company expects to grow revenue by approximately 70 percent in fiscal 2028 and described the outlook as “supply‑constrained.” The current 105.9 percent year‑over‑year growth already exceeds that forward target, suggesting the supply constraint is already limiting what could be an even faster expansion.

Kress highlighted data‑center revenue, noting Q2 data‑center revenue rose 18 percent quarter over quarter to $89 billion. She broke that out into hyperscale and ACIE segments. Hyperscale revenue was $49 billion, up 13 percent sequentially. ACIE revenue was $40 billion, up 25 percent sequentially and 138 percent year over year. The numbers confirm the split: the $89 billion total data‑center figure is the sum of the two segments, and both are accelerating. Management’s emphasis on “strong contributions” from hyperscale aligns with the 13 percent sequential rise, but the larger driver is ACIE, which grew faster and is now roughly half of data‑center revenue.

The company announced an expanded partnership with AWS, adding 2 million GPUs through the second quarter of fiscal 2029. Kress said AWS will serve the NVIDIA Nemotron family on Amazon Bedrock and SageMaker and will adopt the full physical AI stack. While the partnership is a qualitative win, the financial impact is not yet reflected in the quarter’s numbers. The revenue growth narrative is therefore supported by the quantitative results, but the partnership’s contribution will likely appear in future quarters.

Kress also described the rollout of Vera Rubin, the new GPU‑CPU‑networking platform. She said Vera Rubin delivers 30 times higher throughput per megawatt and 35 times lower token cost versus Grace Blackwell Ultra, and that production shipments began earlier this month with orders from every major hyperscaler. The current quarter’s inventory rose to $32 billion as the company prepared for the launch. The inventory increase is consistent with the “preparing for Vera Rubin” comment, and it explains part of the cost‑of‑revenue growth.

On the networking side, Kress noted a record quarter with revenue up 18 percent sequentially, and Spectrum‑X Ethernet grew 2.6 times year over year. The overall gross‑margin stability at 75 percent suggests that the higher cost of components, especially memory, has not yet eroded profitability. However, Kress warned that memory pricing is “extreme” and that margins will dip to about 74 percent in Q3, then to the low‑70s in Q4 before stabilizing in the low‑70s for FY 2028. The guidance aligns with the company’s own forecast that GAAP and non‑GAAP operating expenses will be about $9.2 billion and $9.0 billion respectively in Q3, up from the current expense level.

The balance sheet shows days‑sales‑outstanding at 60 days, up from prior periods, reflecting longer payment terms for large, multi‑quarter purchases. The company returned $26 billion to shareholders this quarter—$20 billion via share repurchases and $6 billion as a dividend of $0.25 per share. That return equals roughly 60 percent of free cash flow year‑to‑date, exceeding the company’s “50 percent or more” target.

Looking ahead, management forecast Q3 total revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2 percent. That would be a sequential increase of roughly 12 percent, driven primarily by ACIE. They also said Vera Rubin will account for about 20 percent of data‑center revenue in Q3. If ACIE continues its 25 percent sequential growth, the revenue mix described by management is plausible.

In the Q&A, Jensen Huang emphasized that compute demand is “extraordinary” and that AI agents consume 15 to 100 times more compute than a human would for the same task. He reiterated that NVIDIA is “the only company in the world that creates and builds an entire AI factory platform.” That claim is supported by the numbers: the company’s operating income and net income grew by more than 120 percent year over year, reflecting a business model that captures a larger slice of the AI infrastructure value chain.

Huang also pointed out that the AI lifecycle now includes four phases—data preparation, pre‑training, post‑training, and agentic inference—and that NVIDIA’s NVLink‑72 rack‑scale architecture underpins all of them. He said each gigawatt of data‑center capacity now represents roughly $40 billion of revenue opportunity, up from $18 billion per gigawatt in the Hopper era. The quarter’s $89 billion data‑center revenue, combined with the $32 billion inventory build‑up for Vera Rubin, suggests the company is positioning to capture that higher per‑gigawatt value.

When pressed on supply, Huang said the company can “confidently deliver 70 percent” of demand and is working to increase that share. The current quarter’s cost‑of‑revenue growth of 17.7 percent quarter over quarter reflects higher component costs and the ramp of new products, but the company’s inventory buildup indicates it is already securing supply ahead of demand.

Huang also highlighted the “revenue‑sharing” model for NeoCloud partners. He explained that NVIDIA provides a take‑or‑pay commitment on a portion of capacity, then shares in revenue above that floor. This model creates a recurring revenue stream on top of one‑time hardware sales. The financial statements do not yet break out that recurring component, but the growing ACIE revenue—$40 billion this quarter—likely contains early signs of that model’s impact.

The discussion also covered the Frontier AI labs. Kress said NVIDIA has invested nearly $50 billion in those labs and partnered with six major capital providers to raise over $500 billion of third‑party capital. She noted that the labs’ compute demand could represent roughly a quarter of NVIDIA’s business next year. While the current quarter’s net income of $59.688 billion already reflects a massive profit base, the additional compute shipments to Frontier labs will add to future revenue and margin dynamics.

Huang warned that memory scarcity is “driven in large part by the AI build‑out itself.” He said the company is working with all three major memory suppliers to increase capacity. That aligns with the margin guidance that expects a dip to 74 percent in Q3 due to memory pricing pressure, before margins recover as price increases are passed through.

In summary, the numbers tell a clear story. Revenue is more than double year over year, operating income and net income have surged over 120 percent, and gross margins remain solid at 75 percent. Management’s narrative of record growth, a supply‑constrained outlook, and a shift toward ACIE and Vera Rubin is reflected in the data. Where management emphasizes future supply constraints, the current cost‑of‑revenue increase and inventory buildup already show the company preparing for that constraint. Where management highlights partnerships and new product launches, the balance sheet and expense trends confirm the investment phase.

For an account executive on the road, the key takeaways are:

- The quarter delivered $96.221 billion in revenue, up 105.9 percent year over year. That growth is already beyond the 70 percent FY 2028 target the company set.
- Operating income of $63.734 billion and net income of $59.688 billion represent more than a 120 percent jump year over year, underscoring the profitability of the AI‑factory model.
- Gross margins are at 75 percent now but are expected to dip to about 74 percent in Q3 before settling in the low‑70s for FY 2028, driven by memory pricing.
- Data‑center revenue is $89 billion, split between $49 billion hyperscale and $40 billion ACIE. ACIE is the faster‑growing half and is projected to drive most of the sequential growth ahead.
- Vera Rubin is in production, inventory is $32 billion, and the company expects Vera Rubin to represent roughly 20 percent of data‑center revenue in Q3.
- The company returned $26 billion to shareholders, exceeding its 50 percent free‑cash‑flow return target.
- Supply constraints are real. The company can currently meet about 70 percent of demand and is actively securing more capacity across GPUs, CPUs, memory and power.
- The revenue‑sharing model for NeoClouds and the Frontier AI lab investments are early signals of a new recurring‑revenue stream that could become material in the next 12‑18 months.
